    Two questions, because one is not enough

    A document stripped of every style, footnote and revision is perfectly self-consistent. The checker asks both, and you usually want both.

The honest claim is not that agents corrupt documents; it is that the variance between pipelines is total and nothing downstream can tell which one you got.     Put it after the step that edits documents

    A generation script, an agent, a template merge — that is where these defects come from, and the only place they are still cheap to find. Two dependencies, Python 3.9+, no model calls, no network.

    What it does not do

    • The evidence is synthetic: 50 sources and 220 labelled pairs across five producers, including files really saved by Word for Windows, Mac and Online. 100 % error-level precision applies to the 14 measured rules on that corpus, not to your documents.
    • Eight agent runs is a small sample, on one document, on one day.
    • Deck layout is validated against PowerPoint for Mac in the editing view. No GPOS kerning or shaping, so complex scripts are estimates. Groups, rotation, tables, SmartArt and charts are reported as unsupported, not approximated.
    • It is not a schema validator, not a malware scanner, not a visual diff. A clean result names what it checked; a result with a gap says no findings in checked surfaces, not clean.

    The next step is a public benchmark of docx editing tools and agent harnesses on a real corpus. If your agents edit Office files that a person then reviews, I would like to hear what breaks for you. Open an issue
